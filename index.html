Anatolian Rock · A field guide

A N A D O L U ROCK

1965 – 1980

A newspaper contest invented it in 1965 and a military coup shut it down in 1980. In the fifteen years between, a generation ran Anatolian folk melodies through fuzz pedals and built a sound that existed nowhere else on earth.

Scroll ↓

How it started

It began in a
newsroom

In 1965 the Turkish daily Hürriyet launched a contest called the Altın Mikrofon, or Golden Microphone. It set one condition for entry: take Turkish music somewhere new using the techniques, forms and instruments of the West.

Seventy-eight acts entered the first year. The paper pressed the top finishers onto 45s and sent them touring through Anatolian cities, where audiences voted by writing a band's name on their ticket stub and dropping it in a box. It ran four times and stopped.

  • 1965Yıldırım Gürses wins with a crooner's ballad, the least modern thing entered. Mavi Işıklar take second.
  • 1966Silüetler take first, Mavi Işıklar second again.
  • 1967Cem Karaca ve Apaşlar place second.
  • 1968The final edition. Haramiler second, Moğollar third, the Erkin Koray Dörtlüsü fourth. Nearly the whole first generation, in one room.
Hürriyet, 1965 · stated aim
To give Turkish music a new direction, by drawing on the technical range and forms of Western music and playing it on Western instruments.
Paraphrased from the paper's announcement of the competition.
The fifteen years

Two clocks
running at once

The music and the politics run here as two columns. One reads as a golden age, the other as a country coming apart. They meet in September 1980.

The music The country
1965

Altın Mikrofon

Hürriyet launches the contest that names the ambition out loud.

Early 60s

A wider political floor

The 1961 constitution opens space for the left and for organised labour. Campuses politicise fast, and "turn toward the people" replaces plain admiration of the West.

1967

Moğollar form in Istanbul

Five musicians set out to write pop in an Anatolian idiom rather than translate someone else's.

1970

Moğollar leave for Paris

They go without a singer, find record company numbers in a café phone book, and land a deal.

1971

The genre gets a name and a prize

Danses et Rythmes de la Turquie, an instrumental record made in France and released at home as Anadolu Pop. It takes the Grand Prix du Disque from the Académie Charles Cros, an award previously given to Hendrix and Pink Floyd.

12 March 1971

Coup by memorandum

The military forces the government out by letter rather than tanks. Martial law follows in much of the country.

1971

Selda Bağcan starts recording

Six singles in her final year at university, folk songs carried by a direct voice and a guitar. National fame almost immediately.

1972

Kurtalan Ekspres

Barış Manço assembles the band he will keep for the rest of his career, named after a train that ran from Istanbul to a town in the southeast.

1974

Elektronik Türküler

Erkin Koray's first album of new material, and the record most people mean when they say Anatolian rock: electric saz, microtonal melody, and a rhythm section that doesn't apologise.

1975

2023

Manço's first real LP. The title track is written for a centenary still fifty years away; "Baykoca Destanı" is among the first Turkish rock operas.

1 May 1977

Taksim Square

Shots are fired into a May Day crowd of hundreds of thousands. Dozens die. Street violence between left and right becomes routine.

1977

Tutkusu

Koray pushes further into progressive territory, records partly in the Netherlands, and tours Europe.

1978

Edirdahan

Cem Karaca names his new band for the country's westernmost and easternmost provinces. By now his lyrics are explicitly about class, and everyone knows it.

1978–79

Toward the edge

Sectarian killings, an economy in crisis, a parliament unable to function.

Early 1979

Karaca goes to Cologne

He leaves on business. He will not be able to come back for eight years.

The end

12 · 09 · 1980

Saying it became
the offence

General Kenan Evren takes power. Parties are shut, and writers, journalists and musicians are among the hundreds of thousands detained in the years that follow. The genre had spent fifteen years learning to say something.

  • Cem KaracaAlready in West Germany when the coup comes, and wanted at home on charges tied to his lyrics. He does not return. His father dies without him there. On 6 January 1983 his citizenship is revoked. An amnesty under Turgut Özal finally brings him home in 1987.
  • Selda BağcanImprisoned three times between 1981 and 1984, the first a forty-day stretch in Metris military prison over a recording. She is acquitted in every case. Her passport is held until 1987, so she cannot tour, and misses an invitation to WOMAD.
  • Erkin KoraySpends much of the period abroad, in the Netherlands, Germany and eventually Canada, still recording and increasingly at a distance from the scene he started.
Who to know

Eight people
and one band

Each one with the record worth starting on.

Erkin Koray

1941–2023 · The first

Playing rock in Istanbul since the late 1950s, before there was an audience for it. He was reportedly attacked in the street over his hair. He electrified the bağlama, which is what put a folk instrument through a rock amplifier. Stubborn, prolific, never quite absorbed into anyone else's scene. Died in Toronto in 2023.

Start with Elektronik Türküler (1974), the track "Cemalim".

Moğollar

Formed 1967 · The exporters

The band that proved the idea worked at album length, and the first to be taken seriously outside Turkey. Their Paris record gave the movement its Turkish name, Anadolu Pop, and a French prize that made everyone at home pay attention. Still performing, more than fifty years on.

Start with Danses et Rythmes de la Turquie (1971), the track "Lorke".

Barış Manço

1943–1999 · The one everyone loved

Long hair, heavy rings, and a gift for writing songs that were ambitious and singable at the same time. He made a rock opera and a nursery-rhyme hit in the same decade. In 1988 he began a television programme for children that ran for ten years and turned him into something closer to a national uncle than a rock star.

Start with 2023 (1975), the track "Baykoca Destanı".

Cem Karaca

1945–2004 · The dangerous one

Born to actor parents, Armenian and Azerbaijani. He cycled through bands (Apaşlar, Kardaşlar, Dervişan, Edirdahan) and called himself a rock ozan, a rock bard. He wrote about workers and apprentices with a theatrical delivery that made the politics land. It cost him his citizenship.

Start with the singles era, the track "Tamirci Çırağı".

Selda Bağcan

b. 1948 · The one who paid

An engineering physics student who became the voice the left sang along to. Folk repertoire, electric arrangements, and a delivery with no ornament in it. The junta jailed her three times and lost every case, then simply kept her passport. Decades later her recordings turned up sampled on hip-hop records.

Start with Selda (1976), the track "Koçero".

Fikret Kızılok

1946–2001 · The quiet one

Came up through the same Altın Mikrofon years and then moved steadily away from volume, toward songwriting stripped back to voice and saz. The introvert's entry point into a loud genre.

Start with the track "Not Defterimden".

Üç Hürel

Three brothers · The craftsmen

Onur, Haldun and Feridun Hürel, working as a trio with a homemade approach to instruments and effects. Warmer and stranger than the big names, and a favourite of collectors for exactly that reason.

Start with the track "Ağlarsa Anam Ağlar".

Edip Akbayram

b. 1950 · The second wave

Arrived through a later revival of the contest format and carried the protest strand of the music into the late seventies with a heavier, more direct band sound.

Start with the track "Aldırma Gönül".

Hands on

The notes between
the keys

Every pitch here is tuned to Turkish makam intervals, which sit in the gaps between the twelve notes of a piano. The riff locks and repeats; the fuzz, phaser and drum kit are the electrification the Altın Mikrofon was asking for. Press play, then move the fuzz.

Afterlife

It came back
from outside

The records kept circulating through crate diggers, reissue labels and samples, and the sound returned to Istanbul partly by way of people who had found it somewhere else. The revival is now larger internationally than the original ever was.

1996

BaBa ZuLa

Founded in Istanbul, folding dub and electronics into the saz. The bridge between the old scene and whatever came next.

2014→

Gaye Su Akyol

Painter and anthropologist turned bandleader. Psychedelia, surf and post-punk under an unmistakably Anatolian melodic sense.

2016→

Altın Gün

Started in Amsterdam by a Dutch bassist who advertised for Turkish musicians. Their 2019 album Gece was nominated for a Grammy.

Ongoing

Derya Yıldırım & Grup Şimşek

Hamburg-based, playing Anatolian folk material with a rock band from outside Turkey entirely.

Notes on sources

Dates and events here are drawn from published accounts of the Altın Mikrofon competition, artist discographies, and reporting on the 1980 coup and its consequences for musicians. Where accounts disagree, which happens often on release years for early singles, the more conservative claim is used or the date is left off.

Turkish spellings are kept as they are. Track picks are opinion.
